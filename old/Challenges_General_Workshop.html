Challenge 1

Speaker Introduction

Writing

You're hosting an AI event at work. The next speaker, Carlos Noyes, is about to give a talk on the importance of AI-training, but he never sent his intro. Generate an accurate, engaging introduction for him.

Use his LinkedIn profile for the needed context. Copy and paste the full profile text into your prompt so the AI has accurate, specific details to work with.

Give it a try first — reveal when you're ready
Example PromptContext: Carlos Noyes is about to speak on the importance of AI-training. Instructions: Using his LinkedIn page, write a 60–90 second, energetic, accurate speaker introduction for him. LinkedIn Profile: [CONTEXT: Paste full LinkedIn profile text]

The key here is giving the AI specific, real context (the LinkedIn text) so it doesn't have to guess or hallucinate details. Structuring the prompt with labeled sections (Context, Instructions, LinkedIn Profile) keeps things clear.

Challenge 2

Trade Show Planning

Reasoning

You are part of a company (pick your own industry, e.g. business consulting). You are leading your company's trade show booth. In years past, there has been limited measurable value in this event. You are going to change that with the ambitious goal of more than 50 qualified leads. The event is in 30 days. What is your plan?

Break it up into smaller steps. Start with ideation — ask for lots of creative ideas first. Then pick your favorites and have the AI flesh those out into full, detailed plans. Don't try to get a finished plan in one prompt.

Give it a try first — reveal when you're ready
Prompt 1 — Context"Summarize everything important about the company I work for: [copy and paste from the website]"
Prompt 2 — Objective + Ideation"I have 30 days to prepare for my company's trade show exhibit. I want to maximize acquiring qualified leads. Think very long and hard, and come up with 10 creative and effective tactics we could employ to optimize our tradeshow outcomes."
Prompt 3 — Plan"I like ideas [x, y, and z]. Think very long and hard, and flesh out a detailed plan for each idea. Include all steps to prepare and execute each of the ideas."

This multi-step approach — context first, then brainstorm, then deep-dive on selected ideas — produces dramatically better results than asking for "a trade show plan" in one shot. Each prompt builds on the previous conversation.

Challenge 3

Finalize the Table

Research

You are gathering data to put in a table for an important presentation. You were able to fill out most of the data from memory, but couldn't remember everything. Use AI to finalize the table — filling in gaps and checking for mistakes. (Use a table of Companies and their CEOs, or similar data you have on hand.)

Don't forget that AI can check for mistakes, not just fill in blanks. Ask it to look for errors in the data you already have. You can upload a screenshot or paste the table directly.

Give it a try first — reveal when you're ready
Example Prompt"Think very long and hard. Fill in any missing data. Check for mistakes and correct any that you find." [CONTEXT: Upload a screenshot or paste your table]

Uploading a screenshot works well here — the AI can parse table structures visually. The instruction to "check for mistakes" is important because your existing data may contain errors, and AI can catch them if you ask.

Challenge 4

Zillow Price Data

Analysis

You are getting ready to sell your house. It currently has 2 bedrooms and 1 bathroom. You are considering adding one more of each to increase its value. To decide if it is worth the cost, you want to know what the average house price per square foot is in your area for both house types (2bd/1ba and 3bd/2ba). Use price data from Zillow to calculate these averages in your zip code.

AI can put messy data into more usable formats. Copy raw listing data from Zillow pages and ask AI to structure it into a table. Then, instead of calculating averages yourself, ask AI to write a Google Sheets formula for you. The "Instant" model may outperform the "Thinking" model for the data structuring step.

Give it a try first — reveal when you're ready
Prompt 1 — Structure Data"Put the following data into a table with headers: price, sqft, bd, and ba: [pasted text from Zillow page 1]" Note: The "Instant" model outperforms the "Thinking" model for this step.
Prompt 2 — More Data"Make another table with the same headers: [pasted text from Zillow page 2]" Repeat for all pages of results.
Prompt 3 — Formulas"I copied the tables into Google Sheets. Can you write me a Google Sheets formula that will calculate the average price per sqft for 2bd/1ba houses? And another formula that calculates average price per sqft for 3bd/2ba houses?"

This challenge demonstrates the power of using AI to structure messy data and then leveraging external tools (Google Sheets) for the final analysis. You don't need AI to do the math itself — just to help you set up the right formulas.

Bonus Challenge

Sales Call Research & Prep Brief

Multiple Skills

Your sales team doesn't have a consistent research or preparation process before sales calls. Some reps skip the step entirely, while others do minimal research with mixed results. As the sales manager, you want to introduce a simple, repeatable, AI-powered workflow that makes sales prep fast, structured, and genuinely useful.

Create a templatized ChatGPT prompt flow that:

  • Organizes all existing information the sales rep already has about the prospect
  • Searches the web for additional relevant information (company news, market context, etc.)
  • Generates a polished, strategic preparation brief the rep can skim before the call

Keep everything in a single thread so the AI can reuse context from each prompt.

In Prompt 1, paste all your raw information at once instead of sending many small messages. The AI is good at organizing messy notes.

Tell ChatGPT explicitly what role it's playing (e.g. "You are my Sales Research & Preparation Partner") to get more focused output.

In Prompt 2, ask it to search the web, but be specific about what you care about (recent news, industry trends, funding, leadership changes, etc.).

In Prompt 3, be very clear about the final deliverable: a brief you can skim in 2–3 minutes, with specific sections (profile, objections, questions, call plan).

Use phrases like "Think very long and hard" to push for deeper analysis. If the output feels off, don't start over — just coach the AI inside the same thread.

Give it a try first — reveal when you're ready
Prompt 1 — Organize ContextI am a sales person preparing for a sales call. Here's all the information I have about my prospect: - Their Name: [Full name] - Organization: [Company name] - LinkedIn text: [copy/paste text from profile] - Transcript or notes from previous interactions: [paste here] - My general notes or observations: [paste/type here] Please synthesize the prospect's information into a cohesive profile.
Prompt 2 — Web ResearchTheir Website URL is: [URL] Please search the web for recent and relevant information about this company and person. Look for: - Company news, leadership announcements, or funding - Industry trends affecting them - Public partnerships or press coverage - Any recent interviews or thought leadership content Please add any new information to the prospect's profile.
Prompt 3 — Prep BriefI am a part of the [Industry] industry, selling [Product]. The goal of my sales call is: [e.g., Schedule a demo / Close the deal / Gather discovery info / Build rapport / etc.] Think very long and hard and create a Sales Call Preparation Document that includes: 1) The Prospect's Profile: A concise summary of all the relevant information of my prospect from our chat history. 2) Objection Handling: A list of potential objections that my prospect may have that considers their unique background and their unique business/industry. Please include strategic objection handling responses. 3) Leading Questions: Generate a list of persuasive, leading questions designed to make someone recognize why they need my product or service.

This is a full Human-AI Sandwich workflow: the human gathers raw inputs (Prompt 1), the AI does heavy research and synthesis (Prompts 2–3), and the human reviews the final brief before the call. The prompts are parameterized with brackets so any rep can reuse the same template.
