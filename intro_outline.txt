Intro Slide 1 — Lesson 1: What Can AI Do?

Slide title:
What Can AI Do? (The 3 Core Capabilities)

A) Generation

AI is extremely good at producing content once you give it direction.

Examples:

Text: emails, reports, summaries, scripts, documentation

Visuals: images, diagrams, slide drafts

Code: scripts, formulas, automations

Audio/video: transcripts, voiceovers, summaries

Key idea to emphasize verbally:

AI doesn’t replace creativity — it removes the blank page.

This is the most obvious use case, and where most people start.

B) Thinking

Modern AI models can reason, not just generate.

What this looks like in practice:

Breaking problems into steps

Comparing tradeoffs

Creating plans and strategies

Debugging logic and spotting inconsistencies

Important nuance:

Some models are optimized for speed and formatting

Others are optimized for reasoning and analysis

You often get better results when you guide the thinking process

Key framing line:

AI works best when you don’t ask for the answer — you ask it to think with you.

C) Agents

AI can now take action, not just give advice.

Examples:

Multi-step workflows (research → summarize → act)

Monitoring systems (check, decide, notify)

Tool use (search, write, schedule, update docs)

Semi-autonomous task execution

Mental model:

Think “junior analyst + intern + automation,” all rolled into one.

This is where AI moves from helpful to transformational.

Intro Slide 2 — Lesson 2: Prompt Engineering

Slide title:
Prompt Engineering: How to Work With AI Effectively

A) How to Start

The simplest and best advice:

Start by talking to AI like you would a capable person.

You don’t need special syntax to begin:

Explain what you’re trying to do

Share what you know

Ask for help

You can always refine later. Most people overthink prompts too early.

B) The Parts of a Well-Structured Prompt

As prompts get more important, structure starts to matter.

Most strong prompts include some combination of:

Context — background information, documents, screenshots

Objective — what “good” looks like

Audience — who the output is for

Instructions — how to approach the task

Constraints — length, tone, format, exclusions

Output format — bullets, tables, steps, etc.

Key takeaway:

The better you set the stage, the better the performance.

C) Tips & Tricks

A few high-leverage habits:

Give the model easy access to context (copy/paste beats paraphrasing)

Break big tasks into stages

Iterate instead of expecting perfection on the first try

Use phrases like “Think very long and hard” on complex tasks

Ask it to summarize or restate context to confirm understanding

Prompting is less about clever wording and more about clear communication.

Intro Slide 3 — Lesson 3: Safety, Security, and Quality Control

Slide title:
Safety & Quality: Using AI Responsibly

A) Hallucinations

AI can confidently produce incorrect information.

Common failure modes:

Invented facts

Outdated data

Confident answers to vague or niche questions

Key insight:

The more you use AI, the better you get at spotting when skepticism is required.

More advanced models reduce this risk, but it never goes to zero.

B) Data Security

Assume anything you paste could be stored or reviewed.

Best practices:

Don’t paste confidential, regulated, or proprietary data unless approved

Be especially careful with:

Client data

Financials

Internal strategy docs

Use enterprise or approved tools when available

Rule of thumb:

If you wouldn’t email it externally, think twice before pasting it.

C) Quality Control & AI-Slop

AI makes it easy to produce a lot of content — not all of it good.

Risks:

Polished but shallow writing

Generic outputs

Over-automation without review

Countermeasures:

Always do the final human pass

Spot-check facts and logic

Prefer structured outputs over long prose

Treat AI like an assistant, not an authority

Final framing line:

AI accelerates output — humans are still responsible for quality.
